O-Bon

2017-08-11T21:00
Ogara: The stalks of hemp that have been stripped of their bark.
It is used to light the welcoming and sending fires of the Urabon festival, and as chopsticks to accompany offerings. (From Weblio Dictionary)

Coarse hemp is used not only in Shinto rituals, but also in Buddhist ceremonies.
Hemp seeds were also used in shichimi togarashi pepper.
The Cannabis Control Act restricts the handling of hemp to fibers, mature stalks and their products, seed collection, and academic research, requiring a license for handling.
Recently, there have been many violations regarding leaves, flowers, and other parts that are prohibited from being traded.
Unfortunately, Hita's coarse hemp has not been approved for use in anything other than Kurume Kasuri shibori dyeing.

Preservation groups that use hemp bark as a dyeing tool discard the stalks, while groups that use hemp stalks for torches discard the bark (fiber).
However, hemp stalks are generally available for sale. I wonder why.

This is a traditional material that has been handed down since before the Jomon period. I wish something could be done about it.

Kurume Kasuri

A long time ago, my mother-in-law made a kimono from a roll of fabric. My wife still wears it often.
Coincidentally, it has a hemp leaf pattern.
I had no idea that coarse hemp was used for this pattern.

Hemp can grow up to four meters in four months, flourishing without requiring much effort.
Because of this, it is an auspicious pattern bearing the wish that babies and children will grow up healthy and strong.
Although hemp is inconspicuous in lacquer bowls, it is an indispensable material to prevent the wood base from cracking.

Amount of Sap per Lacquer Tree

2017-07-17T15:00
In this book, there is a section that summarizes the 16-year lacquer tapping diary left by Shunzo Omori.
In 1996, there were about 400 lacquer trees and the total harvest was about 24 kan (approx. 90 kg). Based on this, the yield per lacquer tree is about 225 grams.

In detail, there were five 5-kan barrels: (7/1 - 8/5), (8/6 - 8/24), (8/25 - 9/14), (9/15 - 10/9), and (10/10 - 10/29). Assuming that Sakari (prime) lacquer is harvested around the time of Obon (by August), 400 lacquer trees are consumed to make two barrels: one for Hatsumeno and one for Sakari.

In recent years, many tappers produce only up to two barrels. It is sobering to calculate the number of trees consumed from the amount of sap produced. The quality labeling on the barrels has also become questionable.
