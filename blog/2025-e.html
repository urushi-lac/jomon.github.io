Book on rural life

2025-03-30T11:30
Before I left Johoji, a publisher offered me the opportunity to write a book. I was fascinated by the idea that if I did well, I might be able to get Writer Jakucho Setouchi to write an obi, so I decided to give it a try.

I wrote the book anyway, a manuscript of 102,232 words, or 256 pages of 400-character manuscript paper equivalent. When my wife read it to me before I presented it to the Writer Jakucho Setouchi, she said the beginning was not interesting, so she made some additions and corrections.

The Writer Jakucho Setouchi pointed out that 'you didn't write this whole manuscript’.
The project was dropped, but after this moment I decided that I had to produce all the lacquer art works myself.

Bowl

2025-04-27T19:00
These dry-lacquer bowls were introduced in the blog post 「Bowl」 on 20 March 2017.

This bowl was made in 2005 and started to be used the following year. The outside has not been repaired, but the inside bottom was damaged once and has been repaired.

The rim has cracked and has been repaired three times. These are the areas indicated by the arrows.
The same type of sap will give a natural colour finish after repair.

My idea is to repair it while retaining the used texture.

Jyakucyou and war

2025-05-28T14:30 2025-06 -07 -08 -09 -10 -11
This is a brochure from an exhibition held at the Tokushima Prefectural Museum of Literature and Calligraphy. I was too far away to go, so I was sent the pamphlet just to have it.

NHK's morning drama 'Anpan' shows just what it is like at this time of year. The pamphlet also mentioned that Jakucho Setouchi believed in the Imperial Rescript on Education, like Nobu, a character in the drama.

I remember when I was in Joboji, my daughter used to watch Anpanman books and videos in the library. For many writers of this generation, the experience of war is at the core of their expression.

Tendai-ji Temple: A World Heritage Site

2025-12-17T17:30
The other day, we held a gallery talk at Nihonbashi Takashimaya.
On the day, Ms Reiko Nagao, former secretary to Ms Jakucho Setouchi, also took the stage. When Ms Nagao recounted the moment when Ms Jakucho told me, ’Let's make Tendai-ji a World Heritage site,’ the vivid atmosphere of that instant filled the entire venue, and I myself felt deeply moved.

This region, home to Tendai-ji Temple, symbolises themes universal to humanity: the inheritance of prayer spanning ten thousand years, the memory of war etched into the history of lacquer, and the vow for peace that Ms Jakucho continually advocated.

The display panels used in this talk, imbued with the aspiration ’Tendai-ji Temple as a World Heritage Site’, will also be exhibited at an exhibition scheduled for next year, again at Nihonbashi Takashimaya.

The next event will be my first held at the “Art Gallery”. As a lacquer artist, and as one entrusted with the wishes of Ms Jakucho, I sincerely hope that as many people as possible will come to appreciate the significance of this place and the beauty of lacquer.
